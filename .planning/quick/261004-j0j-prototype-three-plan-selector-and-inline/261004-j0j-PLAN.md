# Compare three Plan selector prototypes

## Goal

Let the traveler compare three structurally distinct ways to switch Plans and edit the current Plan name from one right-aligned header control, while preserving the authenticated Conversation as the surrounding page context.

## Design question

Which single-control header pattern best replaces the duplicate left “Plans” control and separate current-Plan selector, while making the Plan list fold or slide in from the right, supporting plan switching, and making the name editable inline without a rename button?

## Variants

Render all variants on the existing authenticated Plan Conversation route, selected through `?variant=A|B|C` and a shared floating bottom switcher.

- **A — Right-edge expanding drawer:** One compact current-plan control sits on the right side of the header. Its chevron folds open an animated drawer from the right containing the in-memory Plan list; selecting a row changes the simulated current Plan. Activating the title text enters inline editing.
- **B — Anchored popover with editable title:** One unified right-aligned control uses the Plan title as its editable field and a chevron to open a compact anchored list beneath it. The list stays attached to the control and switches the simulated Plan on selection; title editing is directly in place.
- **C — Sliding selector rail:** The header presents one right-aligned “Plans” affordance with the current title integrated into it. Activating the chevron animates a narrow right-side rail into the header/content edge, with Plan names as the primary navigation; the title can be edited inline from the selected row or collapsed control.

## Constraints

- Treat these as throwaway design prototypes. Keep variant changes isolated from production behavior and preserve the rest of the real authenticated Conversation page.
- Use in-memory stub data/state only for title edits and Plan switching. Show a visible “Prototype — changes are simulated” label; do not call the production rename or Plan mutation APIs.
- Model the product authority rule in the presentation: clearly show a simulated exact-name confirmation step before a changed Plan name is accepted. Do not imply that prototype state is persisted.
- The existing header has both a leading Plans drawer control and a central current-Plan control. Each variant must present exactly one Plan navigation control, aligned on the right; retain Account and Sign out context.
- Make the chevron’s open/close motion visibly fold or slide in from the right, with reduced-motion behavior and usable keyboard/focus states.
- Do not implement or modify the production Plan header, drawer, or rename flow in this prototype task. Wait for the user to select or combine a prototype before production implementation.

## Acceptance criteria

- The existing authenticated Conversation route can render A, B, and C with `?variant=A`, `?variant=B`, and `?variant=C`; changing variants through the floating bar updates the URL and reload restores the selected variant.
- The three alternatives differ in layout, hierarchy, or interaction as described; none is merely a color or copy variation.
- Every variant removes the duplicate Plans control in its prototype view and leaves one right-aligned, working Plan switcher with a chevron motion that folds or slides in from the right.
- A user can switch among at least two in-memory Plans and edit the selected title directly in place without a separate rename button; accepting a title change requires an explicit simulated exact-name confirmation.
- Prototype state is visibly labeled as simulated, stays in memory, and makes no durable Plan mutation API calls.
- The page remains usable at desktop and narrow/mobile widths, with accessible controls, keyboard operation, focus behavior, and reduced-motion support.
- Chrome DevTools screenshots for A, B, and C exist under `artifacts/testing/2026-10-04-plan-selector/plan/` and have been visually inspected for overlap, clipping, and readability.
- The final handoff includes working preview URLs, screenshot links, and tradeoffs, and no production header/drawer/rename implementation is changed before the user chooses a design.
